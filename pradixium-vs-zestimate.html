Pradixium vs. Zestimate: Why We Don't Guess Your Home's Value

An automated valuation model (AVM) like Zillow's Zestimate runs your address through a proprietary formula and hands you back a single number — with no visible reasoning, and no way to check what it's based on. Pradixium Reality Check™ does the opposite: it anchors every figure to a named government or market source you can click and read for yourself, and when the evidence isn't there, it says so instead of guessing.

What this page is not: a claim that Pradixium computes "the true value" of a property better than an AVM. Pradixium doesn't compute an independent valuation at all — that's the point. It checks whether the asking price is supported by real evidence (a government benchmark, a comparable transaction, the property's own rent), and tells you plainly when there isn't enough evidence to check.

Why this actually matters when you're the one buying

A number you can't check is a number you can't trust

An AVM's estimate looks authoritative because it's precise — but precision isn't the same as evidence. If you can't see what it's based on, you can't tell whether it's grounded in this property's real market or just an average that happens to land near it.

Always giving an answer is itself a kind of dishonesty

Real estate markets vary wildly in how well they're documented. A model that always outputs a confident number — even where the underlying records are thin — is optimizing for the appearance of certainty, not for telling you the truth about what's actually known.

The price is only half the decision

Whether you can legally buy as a foreigner, what it actually costs to close, and what happens to your money moving across borders often matter more than a few percent of estimated value — and no valuation model even attempts to answer them.

Pradixium doesn't build its own "independent" valuation algorithm — deliberately.
